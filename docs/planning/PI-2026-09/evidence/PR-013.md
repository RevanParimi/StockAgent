## The four lines, with the card's examples

The Sunday scorecard (email, `GET /portfolio/scorecard`, the Weekly tab) gains, for each
account from its own ledgers:

1. **By exit reason**, after "Closed trades". Last 13 weeks and since the start: closed
   trades, the mean a trade, wins and average win, losses and average loss, then the same for
   each reason. Each trade is counted once, under the reason its **closing** sale was made for:
   a SWITCH is "SWITCH sale" and a TRIM that sold the last share "TRIM", whatever their
   triggers; an EXIT is its first trigger in the advisor's order of checks (stop, case broken,
   price-path exit, shock, crisis, trailing stop); an EXIT on a code with no name shows that
   code (alphabetically first if several); no code is "other (no reason recorded)"; your own
   sale is "sold by you". With the review's eight trades: "Last 13 weeks: 8 closed trades,
   -2.2% a trade (4 won, average +7.5%; 4 lost, average -11.9%). By reason: stop 3, -12.6% a
   trade (0 won; 3 lost, average -12.6%); trailing stop 2, +13.7% a trade …".
2. **Bought back** (the routed FIX-025 line), after "Why we sold": round trips (a sale that
   emptied a position and the buy that reopened it, by any rule or by hand) over the last 4
   weeks and since the start, with the charges recorded on those two rows: "Last 4 weeks: 3 (2
   stocks), ₹430 of charges on those sales and buys back, 1 with none recorded. …". The 4-week
   window replaces the card's "this month", to match the scorecard's other windows.
3. **What waiting cost**, after "Exit rule test". For each decision in which the evidence rule
   kept the stock and the price-path rule sold it (one per holding, as in PR-009: a stock bought
   again is another), the lowest close in the sessions after the review day up to and including
   the 10th and the 30th, against the decision's close, as a fall (0 when never below): median
   and worst, naming the stock. The card's example measured the fall from the buy (₹500 to
   ₹440, 12%); the card's intended result, followed here, measures it from the decision close
   (₹480 to ₹440, 8.3%): the money waiting risked against selling.
4. **Decision pace**: decisions a week over the last 4 weeks, how many in all, and the earliest
   week the switch gate could first pass at that pace and pass two weeks running, "(arithmetic,
   not a forecast)". Example: 6 decisions since 2 Oct, 4 in the last 4 weeks, read 29 Nov: "At
   this pace the gate can first pass in the week of 8 Mar 2027, and two weeks running in the
   week of 15 Mar 2027".

**Prices for line 3.** One Yahoo daily series per stock (`get_price_history`, adjusted for
bonuses, splits and dividends) per scorecard, so the decision's close and the later ones share
one price basis; a dividend counts as received. A per-day NSE-checked close (`stock_quote_on`,
as the gate grades) would cost up to 30 fetches a decision every Sunday. A decision has no
result at a horizon, named with its stock, when the series lacks its close or any session's
close in the window (a missing close could hide the lowest), or when NSE's list could not be
read, is empty, has no dated row, starts after the decision, or lists a bonus, a split, an
unread split or bonus row or a change no ratio converts inside the window, or the registry
records a demerger or relisting there. It is never read as a fall.

## The card's hard-review questions, answered

- **Does any number read a close after its horizon?** No. Line 3 reads the decision's close
  and the sessions in (review day, horizon day], only once the horizon day is on or before the
  week's Sunday; otherwise "short of N sessions". Tests put a ₹80 close on the 31st session and
  one before the decision; neither moves a result (−12%, not −20%).
- **Are re-bought stocks separate holdings, as in PR-009?** Yes: decisions come from
  `first_disagreements` (symbol and buy date). The test's AAA, bought again, is a second
  decision with its own close and window (−20% within 10; 30 pending).
- **Can a trade appear under two reasons?** No: `trade_reason` returns exactly one code, and a
  property test over 200 random ledgers checks the reason counts add up to the closed trades and
  that shuffling a sale's triggers never moves a trade. The line says how many EXITs named more
  than one reason.
