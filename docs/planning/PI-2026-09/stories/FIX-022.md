## What was found (SA-048's review)

**M1, medium: the tax estimate takes the position's average cost, the law each lot's own.**
`core/portfolio/costs.py` `tax_estimate` prices the shares a sale takes at the ledger's
`cost_basis` (the position's adjusted average), and dates them oldest purchase first. For
securities in demat form, section 45(2A) of the Income-tax Act 1961 (CBDT Circular 768) sets
first-in-first-out for both the cost and the period of holding. A secondary source maps it to
section 67(7)(c) of the Income-tax Act 2025; the review did not confirm that mapping. When one
sale takes long-term and short-term shares bought at different prices, the average moves gain
between the two classes, and the year's tax reads high or low:

| Lots | All sold at ₹1,400 on 15 Jul 2026 | Estimate | Lot by lot |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 200 at ₹1,500 (2 Jun 2025), 50 at ₹1,000 (13 Jul 2026) | average ₹1,400: every share gains 0 | ₹0 | long −₹20,000, short +₹20,000; a long-term loss never offsets a short-term gain: **₹4,160** |
| 200 at ₹1,000 (2 Jun 2025), 50 at ₹1,500 (13 Jul 2026) | average ₹1,100: long +₹60,000 (exempt), short +₹15,000 | **₹3,120** | long +₹80,000, short −₹5,000 offsets it, all exempt: ₹0 |

The first row reads low. It also nets a long-term loss against a short-term gain inside one
sale, which `_year_tax` correctly forbids across sales. SA-048's receipt (SA48-D8) said the
average "can move gain between years ... the total over a closed position is the same". The
total gain is the same, but the tax is not. Reproduction:
`analysis_data/sa048_review/test_rv_sa048.py` `test_rv6_*` (ignored folder; the figures are
above).

**L1, low: a CSV import with a missing rate saves half an import.** `core/portfolio/store.py`
`import_csv` saves each holding (`store.add_holding`), then calls `trade_costs` outside the
row's `try`. With a rate missing from `portfolio.costs`, it raises after the first holding was
saved and before any ledger row or `capital_in`. A manual add, a manual delete and the executor
compute the charges before any write, so a missing rate writes nothing there (SA48-D10).
Reproduction: `test_rv4_csv_import_with_a_missing_rate_writes_nothing` (strict xfail) and
`test_rv4_reproduction_of_l1_the_half_import`.

**L2, low: a renamed position loses its buy charges and purchase dates.** The pool of buy charges
and the tax lots are keyed by symbol. An identity reconciliation (`identity_reconcile apply`:
rename, demerger, relisting) replaces the holding with its successor and writes no ledger row.
A later sale of the successor then carries none of the predecessor's buy charges, which stay
listed in `costs.on_open_positions` though nothing is held. Its shares count as short-term "no
purchase on record". Totals still reconcile: equity − capital_in is unchanged. Reproduction:
`test_rv5_a_successor_sale_loses_its_predecessors_buy_charges` (OLDCO ₹59.38).

**I1, info: the exchange-charge comment.** `config.yaml` says "IPFT ₹0.01/crore left out". NSE's
circular NSE/FA/73061 (27 Feb 2026, from 1 Mar 2026), read in the review, gives the cash market
₹306.99 per crore transaction charge plus ₹0.01 to NSE IPFT, ₹307 each side. So the configured
0.00307% is the total, IPFT included. The rate is right; the comment is not.

## Intended result

- **Lot by lot (M1).** Each BUY row is a lot with its own cost per share (its price), its
  purchase date and its deductible charges. A sale takes shares oldest first. Each part's gain is
  its share of the sale value, less its share of the sale's deductible charges, less the lot's
  cost and the lot's charges for those shares. Each part is short- or long-term by its own date.
  A split or bonus scales a lot's shares and cost per share together, as the dates are scaled
  today. Where the lots on record do not cover the sale's shares, or a corporate action other than
  a split changed the cost basis (a demerger's cost fraction, an identity reconciliation), fall
  back to today's average-cost figure for that sale and count it, as
  `sales_without_purchase_date` is counted.
- **No half import (L1).** `import_csv` reads the rates once, before its lock, so a missing rate
  refuses the whole import with nothing written.
- **The successor keeps its predecessor's charges and dates (L2).** When a holding's applied
  identity action names its predecessor (`AppliedCorpAction.key` starts with the old symbol), the
  successor's sale reads the predecessor's pool and lots, scaled by the action's ratio and cost
  fraction. Or, if that does not fit in this card, `on_open_positions` never lists charges of a
  symbol that is not held, and the receipt says where they went.
- **The comment (I1):** "NSE cash market, each side, from 1 Mar 2026: ₹306.99 per crore
  transaction charge + ₹0.01 NSE IPFT = ₹307 (NSE/FA/73061, 27 Feb 2026)".
- Update KT section 7 (the tax method and its example), the autopilot guide's tax paragraph and
  planned list, and guide case 08-F. Add no new framework.

## Acceptance criteria

- Both rows of the table above give the lot-by-lot figures (₹4,160 and ₹0). ACMESOLAR's single
  lot is unchanged (₹7,797.82).
- Over random ledgers, each closed position's lot-by-lot gains add up to its sales' value less
  its buys' cost less its deductible charges, and so equal the average method's total. Only the
  short/long split and the tax can differ.
- A sale with lots that do not cover it falls back and is counted. A split between purchase and
  sale keeps each lot's date and total cost.
- `import_csv` with `portfolio.costs.stt_pct` removed raises and writes nothing (RV4's xfail
  becomes a pass).
- RV5's successor sale carries OLDCO's ₹59.38, or `on_open_positions` reads ₹0 after it, as
  chosen above.
- `test_rv3_*` (the set-off oracle and the loss/gain monotonicity) still pass, with the oracle
  changed to lot cost.
