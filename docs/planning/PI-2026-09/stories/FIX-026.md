# FIX-026 — The analysis names a new stock's sector from our list; an empty or unknown sector is flagged

Priority: **P1** · Scope: **owner decision** of 2026-11-04 (session `stockagent-main-9b`, on
SA-049's design choice T7 in its [implementation receipt](../evidence/SA-049-implementation.md))
· Estimate: **3 points**

Status lives in [STATE.json](../STATE.json), not this card. Depends on [FIX-024](FIX-024.md),
which edits `core/discovery/candidates.py` and `shelf.py` too. **Order:** after
[FIX-025](FIX-025.md), before PR-005. **Recommended before deployment is switched on.** A wrong
sector can work both ways:
- **It can block buys.** "generic" lumps unrelated stocks under one 30% sector cap.
- **It can allow too much.** Two drug makers held under two different keys both pass the cap.

## Owner decision (2026-11-04, "agreed on all")

> "We need to make sure the readings don't give null or error or garbage data here. It's too
> late for some backend developer or tester to realise that. We are equipped with smart ways,
> so let that be vigilant about it (no hard coding, let the same LLM call determine rather
> than static regex)."

## Today (code inspection)

- **How a sector is picked.** A new stock from outside the ticker map gets its sector key
  before its deep dive's LLM call, in `core/discovery/deep_dive.py`. It runs `infer_sector`,
  then `get_orchestrator(sector).analyse`. `infer_sector` reads the registry's ticker map,
  then Yahoo's classification, mapped by two fixed tables (`_YAHOO_INDUSTRY_SECTOR`,
  `_YAHOO_SECTOR_SECTOR` in `sector_from_classification`). Anything else is silently
  `generic`.
- **Stocks shelved earlier.** Those already filed as `generic` take the key their stored
  classification maps to (`candidates.shelf_sector`, SA-049).
- **What the key decides.** The analysis graph (four sectors have native graphs), the
  prediction-store directory a held stock's forecasts are written under, the shelf's
  per-sector cap and SA-050's 30% sector cap.
- **Nothing reports a bad reading.** Nothing says when Yahoo answered nothing, or an industry
  that no table names. The stock is simply `generic`.

## Intended result

- **The deep dive's own LLM call names the sector.** No extra paid call.
  - Its prompt gives the configured sector keys (the registry's list, the one
    `promotion.is_valid_sector` checks) and Yahoo's sector and industry as evidence.
  - Its structured answer names one key, a one-line reason, and whether Yahoo's labels fit.
  - The fixed Yahoo-to-key tables go. They may stay only as a first guess to choose the
    analysis graph; decide and record that.
- **Validated by membership, not by pattern.** The answer must be one of the configured keys.
  `generic` is allowed only as an explicit "none of ours fits", with its reason.
- **Fail-loud data cases.** Each of these is recorded, never silently `generic`:
  - an empty, unknown or malformed answer;
  - Yahoo answering nothing;
  - the LLM saying Yahoo's labels do not fit.
  The idea keeps a named state such as "sector unnamed: the analysis gave 'Pharmaceuticals',
  not one of our keys". Decide, with a reason, whether such an idea may be shelved and bought.
- **Decided once.** The first analysis sets a stock's key; a later analysis never re-keys a
  held or managed stock (its forecast history would split). An idea still unnamed or `generic`
  and not held may be named at its next analysis.
- **Vigilant.**
  - The weekly discovery summary counts named, unnamed, invalid and disputed sectors, naming
    the stocks.
  - A watchdog check warns when an active shelf idea or a holding has no valid key, or
    `generic`, naming them.
  - A test pins every fail-loud case.

## Acceptance

- **A drug maker** with Yahoo "Healthcare / Drug Manufacturers - Specialty & Generic": the
  analysis answers `pharma`, it is shelved as `pharma`, and SA-050's cap counts it with the
  other drug makers.
- **Each fail-loud case** gives its named record, the summary line and the watchdog warning:
  - Yahoo answers nothing, and the analysis still names a key from the company's own
    description: named, with the Yahoo gap recorded;
  - the analysis answers a word that is not a key;
  - the analysis answers nothing;
  - the analysis says Yahoo's labels do not fit.
- **A held stock keeps its key** through a re-analysis that answers another one; the
  disagreement is recorded.
- **No fixed Yahoo-to-key table** decides a stored key.
- **Docs:** KT section 8 (how a stock's sector is set), section 12's row; the guide gains a
  case; CODEBASE.
