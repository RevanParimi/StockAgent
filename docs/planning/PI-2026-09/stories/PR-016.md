# PR-016 — Show what the book earns at prices that could really be had

**2 points** · Lane: **decision path** (it sets the price the record is judged at; fresh-session

Status lives in [STATE.json](../STATE.json), not this card. No dependency. **Order:** after
[PR-013](PR-013.md).

**New conversation? Read first:** [EDGE-ROADMAP.md](../EDGE-ROADMAP.md) sections 1 to 3 and 6 (the
goal, the product in eight lines, the rules for this path).

## Why

The paper book trades at a price nobody could get.

- The 16:30 job on day T reviews the **previous** session:
  `review_date = trading_days_ago(now_ist().date(), 1)` in
  `services/scheduler/python/scheduler.py` (job `rl_daily_review`).
- The autopilot books each trade at that session's 15:30 close. AUTOPILOT_GUIDE: "The 16:30 run on
  day T reviews the previous session and books at its 15:30 IST close", and "The price is the close
  the advisor used".
- FIX-027 and FIX-031 made sure the decision reads nothing from after that close. So the decision
  for session T−1 could have been made before T's open, and the first price to act on it is **T's
  open**. As the job runs today, at 16:30 on T, the first real price is **T+1's open**.

**Example.** Monday closes at ₹100. Tuesday opens at ₹102 and closes at ₹103. Wednesday opens at
₹104. Tuesday's 16:30 run books a BUY at ₹100. A person acting on the same decision pays ₹102 at
best, or ₹104 as the job runs now. The paper book starts 2% to 4% ahead of what could be had.

The KT already says the results "do not establish executable net" returns (spread and slippage are
not modelled). Discovery picks rising stocks, so this gap is likely to flatter buys. Anyone the
owner shows the record to will ask this question first.

## What to build

1. **Two real prices for every autopilot trade**, past and new, from the EOD panel
   (`services/data/stores/eod_store.py`; its columns include `open`, from NSE's bhavcopy):
   - `open_next`: the open of the first session after the booked close;
   - `open_as_run`: the open of the session after the run that booked it.
   If a split, bonus or other corporate action has its ex-date in between, adjust the price to the
   booked close's basis. `core/portfolio/corp_actions.py` does this for holdings, and FIX-016's
   `level_in_basis` in `core/portfolio/exit_shadow.py` does it for exit grading.
2. **A side file**, `data/portfolio/<user>/executable_prices.jsonl`, one row per transaction id.
   The ledger `transactions.jsonl` is never rewritten.
3. **The book's result at each price**: the same trades and quantities, the same costs
   (`core/portfolio/costs.py`), each BUY paying and each SELL receiving the real price.
4. **One scorecard line** (`core/portfolio/results.py` `build_row`; the Sunday email in
   `core/delivery/weekly.py`): "At prices that could be had: the book is +x% (paper +y%). Buys cost
   a% more on average, sells brought b% less. n trades."
5. **The rule, written now.** If the average round trip loses more than 0.5 percentage points at
   `open_next`, or the book's lead over the Nifty changes sign at either price, open a card to book
   trades at the next session's open. Otherwise record "paper prices are close enough" with the
   numbers. The owner may change 0.5 before the first look.

## Acceptance criteria

- An invariant test: a real price never comes from a bar dated on or before the booked close.
- A split on the next session does not show as a 50% gap (fixture).
- A missing bar gives "no price" on the row and a count on the line; nothing is guessed.
- Running it twice adds no row (keyed by transaction id).
- The ledger's bytes are unchanged; the scorecard line carries its n.
- Living documents: KT section 6 or 7 (where the scorecard is described), AUTOPILOT_GUIDE
  section 9, a guide case in TEAM_TESTING_GUIDE.

## Not in this card

Changing the price trades are booked at, or moving the daily run to before the open. Either comes
only if the rule in step 5 says so.
