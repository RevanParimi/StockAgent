# SA-045 — Compute the five universal factors deterministically

Priority: **P1** · Sprint: **4** · Provisional estimate: **5 points**

Status lives in [STATE.json](../STATE.json), not this card. Dependencies: [SA-026](SA-026.md) (lenses, peers, and SA-010's benchmark through it). Design: §4.2 of the [one-engine design](../../../superpowers/specs/2026-09-26-one-engine-sector-lenses-design.md). Added 2026-09-26 at the owner's request (decisions D2, D3).

## Intended result

Today the LLM turns computed numbers back into scores. RSI, MACD, 52-week position and peer P/E are calculated, rendered as text, and then scored 0–1 by the model. This story computes five universal factors in code, the same way for every stock, **in shadow**: they are recorded beside every decision and used by none.

| Factor | Inputs available today | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **Value** | P/E, P/B, peer P/E | 50% lens-peer percentile, 50% own 5-year percentile, both inverted |
| **Quality** | EBITDA margin level and stability | Peer percentile. The factor is marked **partial** until RoE, leverage or cash flow are fetched; add them from yfinance statements if available, otherwise record the gap. |
| **Growth** | Revenue YoY/QoQ, EBITDA trend | Peer percentile |
| **Momentum** | 10-year daily prices, 52-week position, RSI/MACD, bulk deals, FII/DII | 6- and 12-month return relative to the **lens benchmark** (peer percentile), plus trend against the 200-day average |
| **Risk** (higher = safer) | Volatility, drawdown, surveillance flags, promoter holding | Inverse percentile of volatility and drawdown; a surveillance flag caps the score |

Each factor is a 0–1 score with `inputs`, `as_of` (the oldest input date used), `peer_count`, `partial` and `coverage`. With fewer than three usable peers, only the own-history percentile is used; with neither, the factor is **missing**, never 0.5. Inputs older than the lens's staleness limit make the factor missing.

## Acceptance criteria

- The same inputs and `as_of` always give identical factor records (no LLM, no randomness).
- Every factor uses its lens's peers and benchmark. A stock is never compared with another lens's peers, and never with the automobile index by default.
- A missing or stale input gives a missing factor with a reason, never a neutral 0.5.
- For each analysis the factor record is stored beside the decision, keyed by the decision's ID and issue cutoff. It uses no input dated after the cutoff (SA-013/SA-014).
- No live decision, verdict or report changes. Shadow only.

## Unit and integration evidence

- Hand-derived percentiles for small fixtures:
  - a stock with P/E 20 among peers at 10, 15, 25 and 30 gets Value from rank 3 of 5, inverted;
  - a 6-month return of +8% against a benchmark at +3%, among peers at 0%, 2%, 6% and 10%.
- A look-ahead guard: a price or result dated after the cutoff is ignored.
- Missing peers, missing history and stale inputs each produce the documented missing reason.
- Partial Quality is flagged, and coverage reflects it.
- A determinism test: two runs on the same frozen inputs are byte-identical.

Use deterministic fixtures and blocked external transports. Record exact commands, environment, results and any unsupported platform in the evidence receipt. Relevant existing tests plus these independent invariants are required; do not merely assert current implementation outputs.

## Routed input — 2026-09-26 SA-002 implementation (F3)

Some fetcher defaults look like measurements. The factors must read them as missing, not as values:

- `get_financials` substitutes zeros when the revenue or operating-income row is absent. Revenue then renders as `₹0.0Cr` and the EBITDA margin as `0.0%`.
- `get_shareholding` and `get_company_info` show `0.0` for fields yfinance omits (holder percentages, market cap, P/E, P/B).
- `get_peer_correlation` returns correlation 0.0 and beta 1.0 when the index fetch fails.
- The RBI repo rate falls back to a configured value.

SA-002 left the prompt text byte-identical, but the producers now report these structurally. `get_financials()["missing_rows"]`, `missing_fields` on the shareholding and company-info dicts, `default: True` on the correlation, and `fallback: True` on the RBI dict are all additive keys. The data-health `reason` names each one, and a substituted revenue row makes the fundamentals section `fallback`. Factor inputs should use those keys, so a zero from a missing row can never rank a stock. See [the SA-002 receipt](../evidence/SA-002-implementation.md).

**Added by the SA-002 fresh review (2026-09-26, L1):** `get_financials` also uses "Gross Profit" as the EBITDA proxy when "Operating Income" and "EBIT" are both absent. No key or reason records this, so the margin is overstated, and it looks like a measurement. A Quality or Growth factor built on operating margin should treat that proxy as missing, or record which row it used. NaN statement values (review finding M2) are handled in SA-002's rework, not here. See [the SA-002 review](../evidence/SA-002-review.md).

**Added by the SA-002 rework (2026-09-26, M2):** `get_financials()["missing_values"]` maps a present row to the quarter ends where it has no finite figure (NaN, None or infinite), and `as_of` is the newest quarter with every figure. A blank newest quarter makes the section `fallback`. A blank *older* quarter only appears in the reason, as the review specified, yet the derived growth still renders `nan`: QoQ uses the previous quarter, YoY the oldest one read. A Growth factor must read `missing_values` and treat such a growth figure as missing, not as a value.

**Added by the SA-002 re-review (2026-09-26, N1; predates SA-002):** `revenue_yoy_pct` is not year over year. `get_financials` reads the newest `FINANCIALS_LOOKBACK_QUARTERS` (4) quarters and compares the newest with the oldest of them, which is three quarters back. Example: revenue that grows exactly 10% a quarter shows "Revenue YoY: +33.1%", but the real year-over-year change is +46.4%. The analyst's prompt carries that label today. A Growth factor needs the same quarter a year earlier (a fifth quarter, which yfinance often lists only sparsely), and must record when that quarter is missing, or name the figure for what it is. It must not rank stocks on the mislabelled number. See [the SA-002 review](../evidence/SA-002-review.md).

## Routed input — 2026-09-30 SA-008 review (L3; predates SA-008)

`services/data/fetchers/fundamentals.py` has its own `_nse_ticker`. It never used the old
`YF_SYMBOL_OVERRIDES` dict, and it does not use SA-008's instrument registry. So it asks
`{TICKER}.NS` for every ticker. The price history, by contrast, asks the resolved symbol. For
TVSMOTORS the fundamentals ask `TVSMOTORS.NS`, while its prices come from `TVSMOTOR.NS`.
CANARABANK, HEXAWARE and TATAMOTORS are the other registered tickers where the two differ.

SA-008's gate checks only that the technicals' prices came from the resolved symbol. A factor
computed from fundamentals could therefore describe another code, or nothing, while the gate
passes.

Factor inputs should come from fetchers that ask `symbol_resolver.resolve_identity(ticker)`.
Their provenance should record the symbol, as the technicals section does since SA-008. See
[the SA-008 review](../evidence/SA-008-review.md).

## Routed input — 2026-10-02 FIX-003 implementation

A bank's statement has no operating line. `get_financials()["statement_format"]` is now `"bank"`
for one: it has interest income and net interest income, and no operating-income, EBIT or
gross-profit row. Its `quarterly_ebitda_cr` and `ebitda_margin_pct` are `None`, and
`quarterly_net_interest_income_cr` holds its earnings line. Every other statement says
`"standard"`, with the keys as before.

A Quality factor built on operating margin must treat a bank's margin as not applicable, not as
zero. It must not rank a bank's net interest income against another company's operating income.
Examples: YESBANK and FEDERALBNK are bank-format, and so is BAJFINANCE, a lender. PAYTM reports
EBIT and is standard. See [the FIX-003 receipt](../evidence/FIX-003-implementation.md).

## Routed input — 2026-10-05 SA-010 implementation

The SA-002 note above is superseded for the correlation. `get_peer_correlation` no longer returns
correlation 0.0 and beta 1.0 with `default: True`. It needs the index named, and a missing
measurement is `correlation: None, beta: None`, with `unavailable` saying why: no data for
either symbol, fewer than 30 paired daily returns, a flat series or a failed download. A measured
one carries `benchmark` and `sessions` (the paired returns).

A Risk or Momentum factor that uses beta must read `unavailable` as missing. On 5 Oct Yahoo had
no data for `^CNXAUTO` or `^CNXENERGY`, so automobile and renewable-energy stocks have no beta
until SA-026 gives those lenses an index with data. See
[the SA-010 receipt](../evidence/SA-010-implementation.md).
